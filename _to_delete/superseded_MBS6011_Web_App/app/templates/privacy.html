What this system records

Read this before you sit a test. If anything here is not acceptable to you, speak to your lecturer rather than starting the paper.

Your webcam

While a test is running, the page uses your camera to check that one candidate is present and that no phone is being held up to the screen. The video itself never leaves your computer. Frames are analysed in your own browser and discarded.

There is one exception. When the checks raise a flag — a phone in view, nobody in view, or more than one person in view — a single still image is captured at that moment and sent to your lecturer with your name, email and computer number. That image is stored with your submission so the flag can be checked by a person.

Your activity

The system records when you sign in, when you start and submit, every answer you save, and any time the test window is minimised, taken out of full screen, or a screen-capture key is pressed. Each of these is timestamped and printed into your submitted paper.

What a flag means

A recorded incident is a prompt for your lecturer to look. It is not an accusation and it is not acted on automatically. Cameras misread ordinary movement, and reviews are done by a person alongside the invigilator's own notes.

Keeping and deleting

Submissions, incident logs and evidence images are kept with your academic record. Ask your lecturer if you want to know what is held about you.
